💧 AquaSafe — Water Potability Checker

A 2nd-year B.Tech AI/ML project by Vikranth Reddy. A logistic regression model trained on 1,000 synthetic water samples checks whether drinking water is safe from 9 lab readings. 5-fold CV: accuracy = 68.2%, AUC = 0.74 vs a majority-class baseline of 54.8%.

How it works: each reading is standardised (subtract the training mean, divide by the standard deviation), multiplied by the model's learned weight, and summed with a bias; a sigmoid turns the total into a probability of the water being potable. The exact same weights run in this page and in the Python training script (train.py), so the browser result matches the trained model.

Try it: clean tap water (pH 7.0, hardness 180, TDS 15,000, turbidity 3.0) → about 86.6% potable. Murky water (pH 6.2, TDS 45,000, trihalomethanes 110, turbidity 6.2) → about 0.3% potable — do not drink. Typical city supply scores around 46%.

Honest note: this model was trained on synthetic data modelled on the classic water-potability dataset, so it is a classroom demo — a real lab test is the only way to certify drinking water.
